Trivia

Although bitter and pungent, olives are a fruit rather than a vegetable. Olives grow on trees and contain a seed inside, which is a characteristic of a fruit. Like a cherry or mango, olives have a skin, flesh, and a very hard inedible pit. But unlike cherries or mangos, they aren’t meant to be consumed raw. They need to be cured or bribed in order to become edible or milled to produce oil. Olives are typically harvested in the early fall when they are green, but they can also be harvested later in the season when they're ripe and black.
