It was created as a survival food for the poor. At the time, Naples was one of the most crowded cities in Europe, packed with a massive underclass of workers called the lazzaroni. They needed something:
1. Cheap: They had almost no money.
2. Fast & Portable: They worked outdoors and needed a meal they could eat quickly on the street.
3. Calorie-Dense: Flatbread topped with lard or oil provided the energy they needed for manual labor.

While people had eaten flatbreads for centuries, modern pizza officially began when Neapolitans started adding tomatoes.
1. The Fear: Europeans originally thought tomatoes were poisonous.
2. The Solution: Because no one else would eat them, tomatoes were extremely cheap. The starving poor in Naples eventually ignored the "poison" rumors and began layering them on their bread.
3. The First Shops: Street vendors began selling these "pizzas" in the 1700s, with the first dedicated pizzeria, Antica Pizzeria Port’Alba, opening in 1738.

Despite their pungent flavor, olives are botanically fruits because they grow on trees and contain a hard central pit. Similar to cherries or mangos, they consist of skin, flesh, and a seed, yet they cannot be eaten raw; they require curing, brining, or milling into oil to become palatable. These fruits are typically harvested as green in the early fall or as ripe black olives later in the season.

At least 59% of the world population hates olives on pizzas.
